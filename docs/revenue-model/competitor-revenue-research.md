# Competitor Revenue-Model Research Compendium

*Prepared for the SparkPoint revenue-model design effort. Last assembled: June 2026.*

## Intro

This compendium maps how SparkPoint's competitive and benchmark landscape actually makes money, spanning four clusters: DeFi yield aggregators and vault curators (Yearn, Beefy, Morpho and its curators Gauntlet/Steakhouse/Re7, Aave), AI DeFi agents (Giza/ARMA, Almanak, Fungi, Brahma, Axal), chat/trading agents and token launchpads (Bankr, Hey Anon, Griffain, Virtuals, daos.fun), and TradFi/structured-product benchmarks (Numerai, Wealthfront/Betterment, Cega, Ribbon/Aevo, Exponential.fi). The dominant pattern for capital-managing products is a **profit-gated performance fee on realized yield** — roughly 10% at the efficient AI-agent end and 15–20% for higher-touch strategy/structured products — usually collected on-chain via vault-share minting or NAV accrual, often paired with a small (0–2%/yr) management fee and bounded by Morpho's de-facto on-chain caps (50% perf / 5% mgmt). Launchpads and chat/trade agents instead monetize transaction flow (≈1% trade tax or swap fee) routed into token buyback-and-burn, while off-chain robo-advisors anchor a ~0.25%/yr flat-AUM floor. The clearest cross-cluster lessons for SparkPoint: lead with gains-only economics, decide explicitly whether to be the fee-capturing curator or the rail, and build a real fee→token-value path that does not depend on emission-subsidized headline APYs. All dollar figures are point-in-time DeFiLlama snapshots (captured June 2026) and fluctuate; every unverified figure is labeled inline and re-listed in the final section.

## Per-Cluster Detail

### Cluster 1 — DeFi Yield Aggregators & Vault Curators

> **DeFiLlama semantics:** for curators (Gauntlet, Steakhouse, Re7), "Fees" ≈ total yield the curated vaults generate for depositors, "Revenue" ≈ the curator's actual fee cut. For Aave/Yearn/Beefy, "Fees" ≈ gross fees, "Revenue" ≈ the protocol's retained share. All dollar figures are June-2026 point-in-time snapshots.

**Yearn (v2 / v3).** v2 canonical baseline = 2% management + 20% performance (profit-gated at harvest; post-YIP-69 moved to a dynamic structure). v3 is fully configurable per-vault via an "Accountant" contract — managers set their own mgmt/perf fees (default 0 until an Accountant is set), so the commonly cited "0%/10%" is a factory/legacy convention, **not** a protocol-enforced rate. On top, Yearn levies a **Protocol Fee** of 0–50%, taken each time a vault/strategy `report()`s, charged as a percentage of the fees the Accountant collects (not directly on profit). Docs example: 100 profit, 20% perf fee, 10% protocol fee → 18 to vault/strategist, 2 to Yearn Treasury. The v3 Accountant/protocol-fee pattern is the cleanest template for SparkPoint to charge a configurable platform cut atop curator-set fees while staying permissionless.
Sources: [Yearn v3 Protocol Fees](https://docs.yearn.fi/developers/v3/protocol_fees) · [Yearn yVaults](https://docs.yearn.fi/getting-started/products/yvaults/overview) · [YIP-51 fee structure](https://gov.yearn.fi/t/yip-51-set-vault-v2-fee-structure/7752) · [The Defiant – permissionless vaults](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/yearn-permissionless-vaults) · [yearn X](https://x.com/yearnfi/status/1395414725811396612)

**Beefy.** Performance fee on harvested yield: **4.05%** standard (up to **9.5%** max post-BIP:45), plus minor ancillary fees — withdrawal fee ≤0.1% (only on non-"harvest-on-deposit" vaults; redistributed to remaining users, not kept) and a 0.05% ZAP V2 fee. No standard deposit/management fee. Standard split: **3.0% BIFI stakers / 0.5% treasury / 0.5% strategist / 0.05% harvest-caller** (sums to 4.05%). Profit-gated, skimmed on-chain at each harvest (baked into displayed APY). DeFiLlama: TVL ~$113M, fees ~$1.89M/yr, revenue ~$1.79M/yr. Beefy's transparent 4-way split (stakers / treasury / strategist / keeper) is a proven template for distributing autocompounder fees and incentivizing keepers.
Sources: [Beefy Fees Breakdown](https://docs.beefy.finance/ecosystem/beefy-bulletins/beefy-finance-fees-breakdown) · [beefy-docs GitHub raw](https://github.com/beefyfinance/beefy-docs/blob/master/ecosystem/beefy-bulletins/beefy-finance-fees-breakdown.md) · [Beefy Vaults](https://docs.beefy.finance/beefy-products/vaults) · [Beefy Treasury](https://docs.beefy.finance/dao/treasury) · [Beefy on DeFiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/beefy)

**Morpho (protocol fee switch + curated ERC-4626 vaults).** The protocol-level fee switch (Morpho Blue) is governance-activatable, capped at **25% of borrower interest**, but has **NEVER been activated** — it remains 0%. ~$256.69M cumulative protocol fees have been generated, of which token holders received **$0**; a Feb-2025 proposal cited incomplete legal/tax work. (Note: a "~$204M annualized fees" figure cited elsewhere is a DeFiLlama point-in-time snapshot — a different metric from the $256.69M cumulative, not a contradiction.) Vault-level (curator) fees: Vaults V1 = single performance fee capped at 50% of yield, no mgmt fee; Vaults V2 = performance fee capped at 50% of interest + management fee capped at 5%/yr of assets. Collected on-chain by minting new vault shares to the `performanceFeeRecipient`/`managementFeeRecipient` on accrual; V2 changes are timelocked. The flagship critique: the fee switch is off, so MORPHO holders capture none of the economics while curators monetize the rails. Morpho is the canonical "infra protocol vs. curators capture the fees" split — SparkPoint must decide whether to be the curator (capture fees) or the rail (defer to a future fee switch).
Sources: [Morpho Fees docs](https://docs.morpho.org/curate/concepts/fee/) · [Morpho Vault V2 docs](https://docs.morpho.org/learn/concepts/vault-v2/) · [Manage Vault Roles](https://docs.morpho.org/curate/tutorials-v1/roles/) · [futunn/MORPHO analysis](https://news.futunn.com/en/post/70129000/who-is-truly-benefiting-from-morpho-s-prosperity-as-its) · [Token Dispatch](https://www.thetokendispatch.com/p/morpho-is-becoming-the-backend) · [Blockworks Research – Morpho](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/morpho)

**Curators: Gauntlet / Steakhouse / Re7.** Curators run risk config + allocation on ERC-4626 vaults and charge a performance fee (sometimes a mgmt fee), collected on-chain via share-minting to the curator's recipient on each accrual; the general Morpho cap (perf ≤50%, mgmt ≤5%) applies. Pure fee businesses with no native fee-bearing token. Observed rates: **Gauntlet 0–20%** charged on **Native APY only** (not rewards/points), observed at 0% / 5% / 10%; **Steakhouse** flagship vaults at 0% (e.g., USDC) to attract liquidity; **Re7 WETH ~20%** of earned yield. DeFiLlama snapshots: Gauntlet TVL ~$1.488B, fees ~$29.92M/yr, revenue ~$1.11M/yr; Steakhouse TVL ~$1.26B–$2.1B (curator revenue **not cleanly broken out — unverified**); Re7 fees ~$575.9K/yr, revenue ~$89.3K/yr. An aggregate estimate cites curators earning ~$13.83M/yr managing ~$4.4B (**unverified**); total curator TVL on Morpho cited at ~$5.8B–$7B (**range varies by source — unverified**). Criticism: 2025 stress events showed curators earn fees while principal risk sits with depositors, and a "race to 0% fees" to win TVL undermines sustainability. This IS SparkPoint's most direct competitor set; differentiation likely comes via risk transparency, a defensible fee level above the 0% race, and tighter token-fee alignment.
Sources: [Gauntlet VaultBook](https://vaultbook.gauntlet.xyz/vaults/morpho-vaults/incentives-and-performance-fees) · [Gauntlet on DeFiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/gauntlet) · [Steakhouse on DeFiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/steakhouse-financial) · [Re7 on DeFiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/re7-labs) · [Chorus One curators report](https://chorus.one/reports-research/defi-curators-in-2025-navigating-chaos-building-resilience) · [defiprime vaults guide](https://defiprime.com/defi-vaults-guide) · [Steakhouse markets update](https://kitchen.steakhouse.financial/p/defi-markets-update-2026-03-17)

**Aave.** Reserve factor = the % of borrow interest diverted to the Aave treasury, set per-asset by governance (~10–25%; V3 trailing-30d protocol take ≈12.6% of fees). Additional revenue from GHO borrow interest, flash-loan fees, and front-end/Aave Pro swap fees. Borrowers pay (a slice carved before interest reaches suppliers); accrues on-chain to the Collector/treasury. N/A on HWM (spread model). Governance approved a permanent ~$50M/yr AAVE buyback (pilot retired >94,000 AAVE), and the April 2026 "Aave Will Win" vote redirects 100% of Aave-product revenue to the DAO/AAVE. DeFiLlama (Jun 2026): TVL ~$12.97B, fees ~$941M/yr, revenue ~$124.6M/yr; FY2025 fees ~$885M (~52% of all lending-protocol fees, sometimes cited as ~$140M protocol revenue for 2025). Highly sustainable at scale; criticism is reliance on cyclical borrow demand. Aave is the upstream yield source/benchmark — the reserve-factor "spread skim" shows how a base protocol monetizes vs. how a curator layers a performance fee on the same yield.
Sources: [Aave on DeFiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/aave) · [Aave FAQ](https://aave.com/faq) · [Aave V3 docs](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/overview) · [RareSkills rate model](https://rareskills.io/post/aave-interest-rate-model) · [GHO docs](https://aave.com/docs/ecosystem/gho) · [CoinDesk – Aave revenue vote](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/04/13/aave-passes-landmark-vote-ending-months-long-fight-over-who-controls-protocol-revenue) · [CryptoDaily buybacks](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/05/aave-buybacks-protocol-revenue-defi-tokens)

### Cluster 2 — AI DeFi Agents

**Giza / ARMA.** Single performance ("success") fee of **10% on realized yield only** — no deposit, withdrawal, or management fee. (An early third-party blog citing "0.5%" is **incorrect**; both DeFiLlama and Giza's own materials confirm 10%.) Collected on-chain, settled at user withdrawal on realized gains, visible on the ARMA dashboard. De-facto profit-gated; no explicit HWM. GIZA token is the coordinating layer (staking/security/governance/bootstrap) with staking reducing fees; a fixed ratio of fee revenue funds GIZA buybacks/burns (a specific "~0.5% of yields" burn figure is **unverified** from primary materials). Fee revenue is tiny relative to token incentives: DeFiLlama shows annualized revenue ≈$99K, cumulative ≈$125K, recent 7-day revenue ≈$0, TVL collapsed to ~$14K (from a $16M+ peak / $1.3B+ cumulative agentic volume). Classic token-subsidized yield (early "15% fixed APR for 3 months" + 1%-of-supply airdrop); 49.69% of supply (team+investors) begins unlocking in 2026 → dilution risk. The cleanest, most defensible model — profit-only 10% perf fee settled on-chain at withdrawal — but Giza shows fee revenue alone can't sustain the business once subsidies stop.

## Revenue-Model Taxonomy

The distinct revenue models observed across all competitors, with the clearest exemplars:

1. **Performance fee (profit-gated, on realized yield).** A % of harvested/realized yield, charged only on positive gains; collected on-chain via share-minting or NAV accrual. Converges on ~10% at the efficient end and 15–20% for higher-touch strategies. *Exemplars:* Giza/ARMA (10%), Yearn (20% v2; configurable v3), Beefy (4.05–9.5%), Morpho curators (Gauntlet 0–20%, Re7 ~20%), Brahma Morpho Agent (10%), Ribbon/Aevo (10%), Cega (15%), Numerai (20% incentive).

2. **Management / AUM fee.** A flat annual % of assets, charged regardless of performance. *Exemplars:* Yearn v2 (2%), Morpho V2 curator cap (≤5%/yr), Cega/Ribbon (2%/yr), Numerai (1%), Wealthfront/Betterment (0.25%; Betterment Premium 0.65%).

3. **Swap / trade bps (per-transaction fee).** A basis-point fee taken at swap/trade time, on-chain in the pool. *Exemplars:* Bankr (1.2% launch-token swap; per-trade bps unverified), Axal (~1% per autonomous tx), Exponential.fi (~0.2% flow fee — unverified), daos.fun (1% DAO-token trade fee).

4. **Curator fee (curator-set fee on curated/allocated AUM).** Performance (± management) fees set by whoever curates risk config and allocation on a vault, captured on the rails of an underlying protocol. *Exemplars:* Gauntlet, Steakhouse, Re7 (on Morpho); the Almanak creator-marketplace variant where strategy authors set their own fees.

5. **Subscription / usage credits.** Recurring or metered access fees (passes, per-query, "energy"/credits), often priced for predictability and decoupled from token volatility. *Exemplars:* Hey Anon ("Pass" + ~$0.60/query), Griffain (stablecoin-priced "Energy"), Brahma Pro (service/subscription), Bankr SDK (~$0.10/request — unverified).

6. **Token tax / launchpad fee.** A creation/launch fee plus a trading tax on launched tokens, typically split with creators and funneled to buyback-and-burn. *Exemplars:* Virtuals (100 VIRTUAL creation + 1% trade tax, 70/30 creator/treasury), Bankr token launches, daos.fun (launch + carry hybrid), Hey Anon AI-agent launchpad.

7. **Structured-product fee.** Management + performance fee on options/structured vaults, profit-gated at epoch/expiry. *Exemplars:* Cega (2% + 15%), Ribbon/Aevo (2% + 10%).

8. **Carry (creator/manager carry at fund close).** A preset % of net fund profit taken once at expiry of a fixed-term fund, instead of a perpetual fee. *Exemplars:* daos.fun (manager carry at close), Numerai's incentive fee as a hedge-fund analog.

9. **B2B / licensing (infra rake, ratings/data, vault-as-a-service).** Revenue from providing rails, SDKs, or ratings/data to other builders rather than taking a cut of end-user AUM. *Exemplars:* Yearn v3 Protocol Fee (0–50% rake on third-party vaults), Brahma ConsoleKit (agent infra), Exponential.fi (risk-ratings/data licensing — partly unconfirmed), Morpho-as-rails (protocol fee switch, currently off).

Two cross-cutting mechanisms recur alongside the above and shape token-value accrual: **spread/reserve-factor capture** (Aave's reserve factor — a slice of borrow-interest spread, the base-protocol analog of a fee) and **fee→token flywheels** (buyback-and-burn or staker revenue-share — Aave, Virtuals, Bankr, Beefy, Giza, Ribbon's 50/50, Numerai's stake/burn).
